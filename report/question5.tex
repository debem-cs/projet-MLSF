\subsection{Protocol}

\paragraph{Instances.} We used the 30 benchmark instances of the teachers, and 18 formulas from Meowdoku boards. Nine are satisfiable: boards of size $n=10$, 15 and 20, with seeds 1, 2 and 3, produced by the generator of Question~1. Nine are unsatisfiable: boards of size $n=8$, 9 and 10, with the same seeds, in which three cells without a cat form a new region. There are then $n+1$ regions on $n$ rows, and $n+1$ cats cannot be placed with at most one per row.

\paragraph{Heuristics and criteria.} The eight configurations are $H_0$, the random choice of Question~2, and the seven of Question~4. Each pair of instance and heuristic was run once, in its own process, with a limit of 60 seconds, ten runs at a time on a 16-core machine. $H_0$ is random, so its results are one sample. We measure three criteria: the number of decisions, that is the number of executions of the choice of a branch (line 17 of Algorithm~3); the CPU time of the process; and the share of this time spent computing the heuristic. The number of backtracks, which counts the executions of the second branch (line 21), is, in total, at least 97\% of the number of decisions for every heuristic and equal to it on unsatisfiable instances, so it adds nothing to the number of decisions and is not reported. Every answer was checked: the status against the expected one, and every model against all the clauses. None of the 384 runs gave a wrong answer.

\subsection{Analysis}

\paragraph{Are the results in agreement with our expectations?} Partly. We expected the heuristics to beat the random choice, the dynamic versions to beat the static ones, and the heuristics based on clause sizes to beat the simple ones. The first holds on the public UNSAT instances, where H5 and H7 need about 1\,000 decisions against 9\,400 for $H_0$, and 0.5 to 0.6 s of CPU time against 15.3 s. It does not hold on the Meowdoku boards. On the satisfiable ones, $H_0$ solves the seven boards in 2.1 s, faster than every heuristic. On the unsatisfiable ones, every frequency-based heuristic needs more decisions than $H_0$ (33\,000 to 48\,400 against 24\,100), and only H1 does much better, with 1\,400. The second expectation holds on the satisfiable Meowdoku boards, where the dynamic versions need 2\,400 decisions against 33\,200 for the static ones. It fails on the public SAT instances, where H5d needs 57\,900 decisions against 6\,800 for H5s, and on the unsatisfiable Meowdoku boards (48\,400 against 33\,000). The third holds only in part: on the public UNSAT instances H5 and H7 are far better than H1 and H3, and H7s is the fastest version on the public SAT ones, but H3d is faster than H5d and H7d there.

\paragraph{How much does a heuristic improve the initial DPLL?} The gain depends on the family of instances. On the public UNSAT instances, H5 and H7 divide the CPU time by about 25 to 30 (0.5 to 0.6 s against 15.3 s). On the public SAT instances, H7s takes 29.0 s against 70.0 s for $H_0$. On the Meowdoku boards there is no gain in this run: $H_0$ is the fastest on the satisfiable ones and similar to the others on the unsatisfiable ones, except for H1 (1.5 s against 15.3 s). In the number of instances solved, the gain is small: 47 for H5d against 45 for $H_0$, which does not solve \texttt{public-06} and \texttt{public-12}. $H_0$ is a single random run, whose figures vary from one run to another, so these differences are indicative.

\paragraph{Does the dynamic recomputation pay for its cost?} The heuristic accounts for 9 to 11\% of the CPU time in the dynamic versions, against about 3\% in the static ones. The recomputation pays on the satisfiable Meowdoku boards: the dynamic versions need 2\,400 decisions against 33\,200 and are four to six times faster than the static ones (3.4 to 3.8 s against 16.4 to 19.4 s). They also solve all nine boards, which no static version does, and \texttt{public-12}, which neither the static versions nor $H_0$ solve, so they are more robust. It does not pay on the public SAT instances, where H3d, H5d and H7d are slower than their static versions (50.9, 77.3 and 62.7 s against 31.9, 37.0 and 29.0 s), nor on the UNSAT instances, where the times are about equal. No heuristic is the best on all families. By the number of instances solved, which is the first criterion of the final ranking, the best is H5d.
